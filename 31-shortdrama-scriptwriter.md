## SCOPE — WHICH PILLARS THIS GRAMMAR RULES (added 2026-08-12, NIAH_V2 lesson)
Rule 1 ("OPEN INSIDE THE CONFLICT — never establish") is DRAMA/CINEMATIC grammar.
Applied blind to a **vlog**, it deletes the journey: NIAH_V2 cold-opened inside the
cave and never showed the packing, the drive, the arrival — and his verdict was
that the film skips "the process," which is the thing vlog audiences come for.

  car_cinematic · car review · industry value  → rules 1-7 apply as written.
  travel_vlog · daily vlog                     → the JOURNEY IS THE CONTENT.
    Open on an EVENT still (rule 1 survives), but the event must sit INSIDE the
    journey's opening — the door slam, the bag zip, the key turn, the first
    kilometre — not at the destination. The film then runs pack → travel →
    arrive → the thing → the return, and the twist/payoff lands at the
    destination, not at frame zero. planqc 39 enforces the journey beats.
Both grammars still owe: a turn every 8-12s (rule 3), a twist by 40% (rule 4), a
cliff exit (rule 5), reveal-don't-explain (rule 6), emotion as event (rule 7).

## PART A — THE GRAMMAR (seven rules, each stealable by a 20–70s car film)

1. **OPEN INSIDE THE CONFLICT.** K-verticals never establish; episode 1 frame 1 is
   mid-argument, mid-chase, mid-loss. Talyx: shot 0 is the EVENT already underway
   (planqc 9 agrees — this is WHY it's right). pan_borneo's border gantry worked
   because it's a THRESHOLD, not a place.
2. **ONE WANT, ONE OBSTACLE, VISIBLE IN 3 SECONDS.** Not narrated — staged. A
   vertical-drama viewer decides at 3s whether anyone wants anything. Car film
   translation: the car/driver must WANT something (arrive, escape, cross, beat
   the rain) and something must resist (border, mud, dark, distance).
3. **A TURN EVERY 8–12 SECONDS.** The micro-episode's per-ep emotional turn,
   scaled down. A turn = the situation CHANGES (new terrain, new threat, reversal),
   not a new angle on the same situation. pan_borneo: road→river→wildlife→mud→
   beach→night = turns. A cinematic that's 6 angles of one car has zero.
4. **TWIST BEFORE THE 40% MARK.** K-shorts put the reversal early because exits
   cost nothing. The rewind device, a passenger reveal, the destination not being
   the destination. CONTENT.twist names it AND its timestamp.
5. **EXIT ON A CLIFF, NOT A CLOSE.** why_stop is a QUESTION the last shot plants
   ("KUCHING BY DUSK?" is textbook). A resolved ending is a scroll-release; an
   open loop is a rewatch/follow. Series thinking: every video ends aimed at the next.
6. **REVEAL, DON'T EXPLAIN** (the J-short rule). If a card states what the image
   already shows, cut the card. Cards earn their place by adding what the image
   CANNOT say (name, stake, irony — "STILL TOLL-FREE" adds economics to asphalt).
7. **EMOTION IS AN EVENT** (the anime-short rule — Shelter, Puparia). In music-led
   formats the feeling must be STAGED as physical fact: water poured over a face,
   a monkey mid-leap, hands leaving the wheel. If the feeling has no photographable
   moment, it is not in the film.

# PART E — STORY SPINES: WHICH SHAPE, AND WHY
### Added 2026-08-12 on his order: "keep adding knowledge into the planning phase...
### that's why I put the Japanese and Korean reference information there — it's for
### the plan phase to LEARN." This part is teaching material, not a gate.

The seat picks ONE spine per film and names it in the plan. Two exist, and the
choice is not taste — it follows from whether the film has an ANTAGONIST.

## E1 · THREE-ACT (Western, conflict-driven)
```
ACT I  SETUP        who, where, what they want, what resists      ~25%
ACT II CONFRONTATION obstacles escalate, the want costs something ~50%
ACT III RESOLUTION   the want is won or lost, and it MEANS something ~25%
```
Engine: **escalating conflict**. The character is PROACTIVE — they push, the world
pushes back, harder each time.
Use for: **car review** (the claim → the doubt → the proof), **industry value**
(the problem → the false fixes → the real one). Anything with an opponent, a
price, a decision to argue.

## E2 · KISHŌTENKETSU 起承転結 (Japanese/Korean/Chinese, discovery-driven)
```
KI  起 introduction   establish the world, quietly. No conflict needed.
SHŌ 承 development    the same world, deeper. Still no clash.
TEN 転 THE TURN       an unforeseen element that RE-FRAMES everything before it
KETSU 結 conclusion   the new understanding and the old world, merged
```
Engine: **the TEN** — tension comes from DISCOVERY, not from a clash. There is no
antagonist. The character is RESPONSIVE: they go about their business until
something forces them to see differently. The structure is deliberately
asymmetric — it holds its biggest element back to the THIRD of four acts, and
that element often changes what genre the story was.

**This is Talyx's native spine and it took a session to notice.** A cave has no
antagonist. A road has no antagonist. A car being admired has no antagonist.
Forcing three-act conflict onto them produces fake stakes; kishōtenketsu produces
the thing the footage can actually deliver — a reveal that re-reads everything
before it.

NIAH mapped, as the worked example:
```
KI    the beam finds a cricket in the dark; the morning trail    (world, quiet)
SHŌ   the plankwalk, the massif, the door, the descent           (same world, deeper)
TEN   "THOSE AREN'T STARS." the ceiling is alive                 (RE-FRAMES the dark)
KETSU the mountain empties; the awe lands on his face; the sky   (the merge)
```
The TEN is what makes the KI re-read: the first shot was never "an insect," it
was "the dark is inhabited," and the viewer only learns that at the turn.

## E3 · WHICH ONE — the decision rule
| the film has… | spine | the twist is… |
|---|---|---|
| an opponent, a price, a claim to defend | THREE-ACT | the proof that settles it |
| a place, a machine, a day, a feeling | **KISHŌTENKETSU** | a re-framing of what was already on screen |
| a journey (vlog) | KTK **inside** the journey | at the destination, never at frame zero |

Vlog note (see SCOPE above): the journey supplies the KI and SHŌ — packing and
travelling ARE the establishing acts, and the audience watches them for the
process, not despite it. The TEN waits at the destination.

# PART F — THE TRIPLE LINK: what must be visible BEFORE generation
### His words, 2026-08-12: "the link between scenes seen beforehand, before video
### generation… I need there to be like three link. That's storytelling."

A cut is not a join, it is a SENTENCE. Every boundary in the plan declares **three
links**, and all three are written before a credit is spent:

```
1 PICTURE LINK   something the eye carries across the cut
                 exit motion -> entry motion · a shape rhymed · a colour held ·
                 the same object seen closer · light continuing its arc
2 SOUND LINK     something the ear carries across the cut
                 a sound that starts in A and finishes in B · a space that opens
                 or closes · a level that steps deliberately · a silence prepared
3 STORY LINK     what the second shot means BECAUSE of the first
                 consequence ("so…") · answer ("what he was looking at") ·
                 escalation ("and worse") · re-frame ("and it was never that")
```
A boundary with only a picture link is a **transition**. A boundary with all three
is a **story beat**. A film made of transitions is a showreel; a film made of
story beats is a film.

**THE READ-ALOUD TEST (free, run it at plan review):** write the STORY LINKS in
order, one line each, and read them as continuous prose. If they form a paragraph
that makes sense to someone who has not seen the footage, the film has a spine. If
they read as a list of pretty things, the order is wrong — fix it in the plan,
where reordering is free, not after generation, where it is not.

The existing `LINKAGE` block is the story-link column. Picture and sound links
belong beside it — declare them in the plan's linkage notes and, once the
storyboard shows them, the whole chain is inspectable before spend. (Candidate,
HIS CALL: `tools/storyboard.py` renders the three links under each boundary; and
planqc could require sound links wherever FOLEY declares a foreground line.)

# PART G — WHAT THE PLANNER MUST LEARN FROM K/J SHORT DRAMA (working list)
Additive knowledge, kept growing. Nothing here is a gate.

1. **The turn is contextual, not confrontational.** A reveal that re-frames beats
   a fight the footage cannot stage.
2. **Hold the biggest element to the third act, not the first.** The cold open
   shows a PROMISE of it; the thing itself waits.
3. **Characters respond.** Nev does not conquer the cave; the cave shows him
   something and his FACE is where the story lands. Reaction is content.
4. **Episodes end aimed at the next.** Every K-vertical ends on an unanswered
   door. Ours: "DEATH SHIPS. NEXT CAVE."
5. **Mundane setting, outsized feeling** (slice-of-life grammar): the plankwalk
   is ordinary; the sweat, the breath and the look up are what make it a scene.
6. **One object can carry the whole backstory** (Shelter/anime-short grammar):
   the headlamp, the boat, the bag. Give one object a life across the film.
7. **Wordless emotion beats narrated emotion** in music-led formats — which is
   why the pillar is BGM-led and the cards say only what the image cannot.
8. **Restraint is a technique** (J-short/festival): the audience assembles the
   story from shown parts. Cutting a card that explains is an upgrade, not a loss.

## H2 · EVERY PILLAR OPENS DIFFERENTLY — and one contradicts our own doctrine
- **car_cinematic opens STILL and holds.** First cut at a median of 4.07s; opening
  motion measures 0.08–0.88× the film's own average. These films breathe for 3–6
  seconds before the first cut. Our planqc 9 demands an EVENT hook under 2.0s —
  inherited from i8 retention doctrine, and **the pillar's own references do not
  do it.** Recorded in `refsense.json` as a candidate re-derivation. Not silently

- **travel_vlog opens on a small human action** — sitting up, a curtain, a phone
  picked up. Not an event. The event is the ROUTINE.
- **car_review / industry open on a CLAIM**, stated as a graphic in frame 1.

## H3 · THE PROCESS MECHANIC, FOUND IN THE REFERENCES (his note, confirmed)
Two of the six travel_vlog references are Korean lifestyle/study vlogs, and both
run their first third on GETTING READY. Their spine is a device we have never
used: **on-screen TIMESTAMPS advancing** — `7:00 → 7:15 → 7:36 → 8:00`, one small
caption per shot. The clock IS the story; the audience is watching time pass in
order. A study vlog does it with a timer app on screen; a travel vlog does it with
departure times. **This is the cheapest possible fix for the journey problem: one
caption per shot, zero extra generation.**

## H4 · TEXT IS A FIRST-CLASS GRAPHIC, AND ITS REGISTER IS PER-PILLAR
| pillar | how text appears |
|---|---|
| travel_vlog | tiny lowercase, lower-mid, delicate (`morning`, `quiet like`) + timestamps |
| car_cinematic | HUGE display type filling the frame, letters cropped by the frame edge — a design element, never a subtitle |
| car_review | white rounded card, top-left, frame 1: subject + price (`Hyundai Palisade $49,070`) |
| industry | word-by-word karaoke with ONE word highlighted yellow per line, plus big yellow number blocks (`Estimated cost: $11,356,920`) |

Our `capcards.py` style (white + one #FFD54A keyword on a scrim) is **the industry
pillar's grammar**, and the reference set confirms it independently. But it is the
WRONG register for travel_vlog, whose refs whisper. Candidate: a `quiet` caption
style — small, lowercase, low opacity, plus the timestamp variant.

## H5 · WHO TALKS
Presenter-led with speech captions: car_review, industry. Music-led with no voice:
car_cinematic, travel_vlog. Our BGM-led no-VO pillar choice is correct for the two
we make — and if a talking pillar ever ships, its captions are karaoke, not cards.

## I3 · THE TEST TO RUN BEFORE HANDING HIM A BOARD
Read the board as if you were him, and ask of every layer: **can I SEE this decision

invisible, the board is not ready — BOARD QC now fails most of these, and anything
it cannot check yet is a check waiting to be written.
