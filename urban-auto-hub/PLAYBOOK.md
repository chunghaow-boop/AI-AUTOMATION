## Hook styles (first 0–3 seconds)

1. **Price shock** — the number appears before the car. "RM___. Berbaloi ke?"
2. **Unanswered question** — something covered/hidden in frame 1 (thumb on the grade,
   cloth on the badge). Viewer stays to see it revealed
3. **Bold claim** — "Feature ni bukan luxury — kat Sabah ini keperluan."
4. **Everything on the table** — documents laid out before a word is spoken (transparency
   hook)
5. **Cold sound** — no music, one real sound: engine start, door thunk, key drop
6. **Mistake framing** — "3 benda ramai terlepas bila beli recond" (teaches, never
   accuses)
7. **Split screen** — two cars, two prices, one question in frame 1
8. **Mid-action start** — video begins in the middle of something (handover, inspection),
   no intro
9. **Direct challenge** — "Kalau korang tak boleh jawab ni, jangan beli kereta lagi."
10. **Unexpected character** — someone you don't expect talking about cars (the cleaner,
    the mum, the kid)

## Twist types (the turn in the middle)

1. **Music cut to silence** — the page's signature. Hard cut, never fade
2. **Good-news-then-flaw** — build the car up, then show one real defect calmly
3. **Role reversal** — the "customer" turns out to be the expert, or vice versa
4. **Price reveal delay** — everything shown first, price only at the end
5. **Expectation flip** — set up what looks like a sales pitch, deliver a lesson instead
6. **Comparison flip** — the "obvious" winner loses on one detail that matters

## CTA styles (the ending)

1. **Question CTA** — end on a real question; the comment section is the goal
2. **Connect CTA** — "WhatsApp kami, kami arrange dengan dealer" (the standard)
3. **Save CTA** — "Simpan video ni sebelum korang pergi tengok kereta" (education videos)
4. **Choose CTA** — "Comment nombor kereta yang korang nak" (carousel/stock)
5. **Next-episode CTA** — "Esok kami tunjuk [X]. Follow supaya tak terlepas."
6. **Send-to CTA** — "Hantar video ni kat kawan yang tengah cari kereta"

## Content styles (rotate across the week)

1. **Industry value** — teach something buyers genuinely need (auction sheets, loans,
   inspections)
2. **Transparency** — documents and flaws on camera; the trust builder
3. **Emotional** — human moments: handover day, family, the mother post that got 1,000
   views
4. **Comedy/skit** — allowed and encouraged, ONE rule: the joke is never at a dealer's or
   customer's expense. Laugh WITH the buying experience, never AT anyone
5. **Spectacle** — sound, motion, satisfying visuals (cold start ASMR, detailing)
6. **Comparison** — two options, one honest question

## Style rules that never change

- Comedy never breaks the no-accusation rule — a funny "dealer tipu" skit is still banned
- Every experiment still passes the compliance checklist (real prices, no sold units,
  dealer named)
- Whatever the style, the identity line and the dealer-routed CTA stay in
