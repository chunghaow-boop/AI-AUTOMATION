Ahad · 30 Ogos // Jumpa owner baru minggu ni

Photo carousel + poll · 2–4 cards · no video · posted Sunday evening
Hook #2 Unanswered questionCTA Question · pollStyle Emotional · social proof
ROLE BOUNDARY. The dealer sold the car. We introduced the buyer. Wording is fixed: “__ unit dari dealer partner kami jumpa owner baru” and “Kami yang arrange, dealer yang serah kunci.” Never “kami jual”, never “kami dah sold”, never a sales figure presented as ours.

What this video has to do

Two jobs. Show the page has momentum, which it needs after a quiet month. And harvest next week's direction from the audience instead of guessing — the poll result feeds straight into next Sunday's plan.

Before you shoot

  • Ask the dealer which units actually found owners this week and get his permission to show each one.
  • Ask separately about the buyers: no faces, no names, no plates unless the buyer has explicitly said yes. When in doubt, shoot the car alone.
  • If nothing sold this week, do not invent it. Run the poll on its own with a line about a quiet week — honesty here costs nothing and protects everything.
  • Write the poll options before you build the cards. Three options maximum.

Gear & setup

  • Whatever you already shot this week — handover moments, cars leaving the lot, keys changing hands
  • If you have nothing, shoot the empty space where the car was. That reads better than a stock graphic.
  • Keep it warm: late light, no heavy grading. This is the emotional register that has performed best on this page.

Lines that must land exactly

  • “__ unit dari dealer partner kami jumpa owner baru minggu ni.” — this phrasing, not a paraphrase.
  • “Kami yang arrange, dealer yang serah kunci.” — the boundary line for this post.
  • No buyer faces, names or plates without explicit consent.
  • If nothing sold, say so plainly and run the poll alone. Never invent a number.
  • The poll winner must actually appear in next week’s plan.

Everything else marked SAY IT YOUR WAY is intent, not wording. Nev should paraphrase in his own mouth — reading it verbatim is what makes it sound stiff.
